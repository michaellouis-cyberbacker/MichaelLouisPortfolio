Market Updates: 60 Seconds

 OAKLANDSFBAY AREA
Median sale price$955K$2.39M$1.38M
Change vs. last year−2.8%+29.2%+5.8%
Oakland: The median price dipped 2.8% from last year to $955K, but homes are still moving fast: 14 days on market and sales at about 118.6% of list price. Active listings are down 23.7% to 322, and 26.1% of them have cut their price.

San Francisco: The median jumped 29.2% to $2.39M, the biggest annual gain of any county in California. Medians can swing with the mix of homes that sell.

Bay Area: It was the only region where sales rose from September (+1.9%) and it has the tightest supply in the state at 2.4 months, even though sales are down 12.1% from a year ago.

California: Sales fell 12.7% from a year ago to the slowest pace in nearly three years, while the median price held steady at $886,760 and homes sold at 98.5% of list price.
Oakland at a glance
INVENTORY
3.8 mo.
California’s supply rose to 3.8 months from 3.2 a year ago, and active listings are up 6.6% from last year. More choices for buyers, but the Bay Area is still the tightest region at 2.4 months.
 
MORTGAGE RATES
7.21%
The average 30 year fixed rate was 7.21% for the week ending Nov. 25, down from 7.28% the week before. It averaged 7.33% in October, up from 6.86% in September.
 
My take

November shows two markets at once. With rates above 7%, buyers are being choosy and sales are slowing across California, but Bay Area prices are holding up, and well priced Oakland homes are still selling above list. For buyers, knowing your numbers and being ready to move matters, and price cuts on Oakland listings can mean room to negotiate. For sellers, pricing and presentation still decide how fast you sell.

Sources: California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) October 2026 reports for Oakland, San Francisco County, the Bay Area and California; Freddie Mac weekly survey. Oakland is a city figure, San Francisco is the county, and the Bay Area is C.A.R.’s nine-county region.

Prop 37 Passed: A New Down Payment Program for Buyers

California voters approved Proposition 37 on Nov. 3. It lets the California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA) sell up to $25 billion in revenue bonds to fund a new down payment assistance program for middle-income buyers of qualifying new homes. The Associated Press called the race on election night, and results become official when certified on Dec. 11.

DOWN

minimum down payment from the buyer

BONDS

•Who it’s for: households earning up to double the area median income who can’t save a large down payment.
•The rules: buyers must live in the home, qualify for a mortgage, and work with a licensed California real estate agent or broker.
•Who pays: participating buyers repay the loans, not the state’s general fund. The Legislative Analyst found no direct state or local costs.

The program still has to be set up, so there’s no start date yet, and it covers new construction rather than resale homes. The Legislative Analyst listed a few open questions: how much investors want the bonds, how the loans’ cost compares with other assistance, and whether it leads to more homebuilding and homebuying.

Fit Realtor Corner

90 Minutes a Week

A new Harvard-led study followed more than 147,000 adults for up to 30 years. People who did about 90 to 119 minutes of strength training a week had these lower risks than people who did none:

OVERALL
13%
lower risk of death from any cause
 
HEART
19%
lower risk of dying from heart disease
 
BRAIN
27%
lower risk of dying from neurological disease
•Pair it with cardio. People who did both had up to a 45% lower risk than those who did little cardio and no strength work.
•More isn’t always better. The benefit leveled off at about 120 minutes a week.
•Start simple. Bodyweight squats, push-ups and step-ups work, then add bands or light dumbbells.
•Keep it doable. 45 minutes twice a week or 15 minutes a day both count. The best routine is the one you stick with.
This was an observational study, so it shows a link, not proof. Check with your doctor before starting a new routine. Source: Harvard Health.
