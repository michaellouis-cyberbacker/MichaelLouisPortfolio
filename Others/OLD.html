Market Updates 📊


🏡 Market Snapshot — October Edition

Median sale price in Oakland: ~ $773,000 (up ~3.0% year over year). Well priced homes continue to attract strong interest, even as higher mortgage rates challenge affordability.

Median sale price in San Francisco: ~ $1,579,000 (up ~14.9% year over year). Limited inventory and strong demand continue to support home prices.

Days on market:

In the East Bay, homes are still moving quickly. Oakland homes sold in a median of about 21 days in September.

San Francisco remains even more competitive, with homes selling in a median of about 17 days.

List to sale dynamics:

San Francisco continues to see strong competition, with nearly 70% of homes selling above asking price.

Oakland remains competitive too, with about 66% selling above list price. Buyers still have opportunities when a home needs work or is priced too high.

Inventory trends:

California's housing inventory held steady at 3.7 months of supply in September, with more active listings as buyer demand slowed.

Across the Bay Area, inventory remains limited in many neighborhoods, keeping competition strong for desirable homes.

———————————————————————————————————

My take:

October is showing us that even with higher mortgage rates, buyers are still competing for the right homes. Oakland continues to offer opportunities depending on the neighborhood and condition, while San Francisco remains especially competitive. For buyers, being prepared and knowing when to negotiate matters. For sellers, pricing and presentation are still key. The market is active, but buyers are paying close attention to value.

Mortage rates

Nationwide, the average 30 year fixed mortgage rate is around 7.32%, down slightly from 7.40% the previous week. Higher borrowing costs continue to impact affordability and are contributing to slower home sales as we move further into the fall market.

 

Policy Spotlight 🔦




🌱 Bay Area Cities Ranked the Greenest in America!

Here's something pretty cool about the Bay Area. According to WalletHub's 2026 rankings, Oakland, San Jose, and San Francisco took the top three spots among the 100 largest U.S. cities for environmental sustainability. Fremont also made the top 10, coming in at number seven!

Oakland ranked #1, standing out for its air quality, low greenhouse gas emissions, parks, and access to bike paths.

San Jose came in #2, thanks in part to its solar energy use, with more than 50% of its electricity coming from renewable sources.

San Francisco ranked #3, leading the country in community gardens and local food programs while also standing out for water conservation and public transportation.

The rankings looked at everything from air quality and renewable energy to transportation and green spaces. It's another reminder of what makes living in the Bay Area special, beyond just the homes and neighborhoods.
